Time-Integrity Field Manual · No. 01

The Silent
Shift.

A delay attack does not break your network. It does not drop a packet, forge a signature, or trip an alarm. It moves a clock by half of a delay it inserts — and the math says you cannot see it from the wire. This is the story of that half, and of the signal beneath the network that finally catches it.

I

Clocks in Agreement

Before the attack: three assets, one shared sense of "now." This is what correct looks like — and why it's fragile.

PANEL 01 · NOMINAL EXCHANGE
Story

The base, the jet, and the boat all agree on the time to the microsecond. A strike is timed, a rendezvous is set, and everyone's picture of the world lines up. Nothing here looks remarkable — which is exactly the point.

System

PTP trades four timestamps per exchange: the master stamps a message out (t1) and in (t4); the slave stamps it in (t2) and out (t3). From those four numbers each slave computes its own offset and steers its clock onto the master's.

Algebra
t2 − t1 = d ms + θ
t4 − t3 = d sm − θ
θ̂ = ½[(t2−t1)−(t4−t3)] = θ + ½(d ms−d sm)

If the path is symmetric (d ms = d sm), the delay terms cancel and θ̂ = θ. The estimate is exact. That single assumption is the whole game.

Correct rests on one word: symmetric.

The offset solver never measures each direction separately — it can't. It assumes the trip out and the trip back take equal time, and splits the difference. Break that assumption quietly, and the solver will keep reporting "all clear" while it walks the clock off a cliff.

II

The Silent Shift

An adversary slips onto one link and adds delay to a single direction. No packet is lost. No signature fails. Watch the clock move anyway.

PANEL 02 · THE BOX ON THE WIRE
Story

Somewhere on the link — a compromised switch, a tapped fiber, a rogue relay — sits a box. It lets messages toward the jet through untouched, but holds each reply for a few extra microseconds before releasing it. It never alters content. It only waits.

System

This is the undetectable delay box. Encryption doesn't help: the bytes are authentic and intact. Path-asymmetry checks that need a second route don't help a single-homed node. The box attacks when a valid message arrives, not what it says.

Algebra
d sm → d sm + δ

One direction's delay grows by δ. The forward direction is untouched. Nothing in the payload changes — only the arrival time of a message that is otherwise perfectly valid.

PANEL 03 · THE CLOCK IS PULLED
Story

The jet's clock is now fast by half the inserted delay. Not broken — shifted. Every log says healthy. Every status light is green. The jet believes, with total confidence, in a "now" that is quietly wrong. This is the shift the manual is named for.

System

Because the solver splits total round-trip delay evenly, an imbalance of δ lands as an offset error of exactly δ/2. Worse: the mean-delay estimate rises by δ/2 too — which just reads as "a slightly longer route." Entirely plausible. Entirely unflagged.

Algebra
θ̂ = θ + ½(d ms − d sm − δ) = θ − δ/2*
d̂ = ½[(t2−t1)+(t4−t3)] = d + δ/2

*Sign of the pull follows which direction is delayed; magnitude is always δ/2. The excess delay hides inside a larger, innocuous-looking path estimate.

PANEL 04 · WHY THE WIRE CAN'T SEE IT
Story

You might think a smarter algorithm could catch this. It can't — and that's a theorem, not a shortcoming. From inside the network, a delay imbalance and a clock error are the same measurement. The wire physically cannot tell them apart.

System

Three things are unknown — the offset and the two one-way delays — but the timestamps give only two independent combinations. The system is underdetermined by one dimension. Estimation theory (Freris–Graham–Kumar) proves the antisymmetric delay is unrecoverable from the node's own observables.

Algebra
unknowns: {θ, d ms, d sm} — dim 3
observables: 2 independent sums
→ blind subspace dim = n − 1

The defense the wire needs does not exist in the wire. This is the settled premise — not a wall to engineer around, but the reason a second, outside reference is required.

III

The Cost

The shift is silent — but it is not harmless. Follow the same δ/2 into three real operations and watch what it actually breaks. The rule underneath all three: a clock error becomes a physical error at the speed of the signal.

PANEL 05 · BASE — THE EMITTER MOVES 9 KILOMETRES
Story

The base runs passive geolocation — separated antennas listening for a hostile emitter and triangulating it from the tiny differences in when its signal arrives. The delay box has pulled one antenna's clock. The computed position slides kilometres off truth. A strike cued on that fix lands on empty ground — or worse.

System

Time-difference-of-arrival turns a clock error straight into a position error at the speed of light: about 300 m per microsecond. A 30-µs pull is ~9 km before geometry (GDOP) makes it worse. TDOA is designed to sync to nanoseconds; a microsecond-class offset is already catastrophic.

Algebra
Δx ≈ c · Δt = 3×10⁸ · 30µs = 9 km
Δx real = GDOP × c·Δt (GDOP > 1)

Same physics hits the base's own grid: a PMU crosses the 1% error limit at 26.5 µs = 1° — enough to false-trip a generator.

9 km geolocation error @ 30 µs
×GDOP geometry amplifies it
ns-class its real sync budget
26.5 µs PMU 1° false-trip line
PANEL 06 · AIR — THE JET FALLS OUT OF THE NET
Story

The jet lives on Link 16, sharing the picture and taking cues. As its clock is pulled it first loses the fine sync its precision navigation depends on. Push the offset toward a slot-width and it transmits into someone else's time slot — and drops off the shared picture entirely. Its jam-resistant radio, whose hops are keyed to time, desyncs and the link dies.

System

Link 16 is TDMA: 7.8125 ms slots, 128 a second. Fine sync fails at a few tens of microseconds; the relative-navigation function needs under 20 ns. Frequency-hop TRANSEC derives the hop pattern from time-of-day, so drift past a dwell fraction desynchronizes the sequence and the link fails.

Algebra
slot = 7.8125 ms · fine-sync window ≈ 50 µs
nav function needs |Δt| < 20 ns
|Δt| > dwell ⇒ hop desync

Tens of µs degrades; approaching a slot-width drops the terminal from the net.

7.8125 ms TDMA slot width
~50 µs fine-sync window
<20 ns relative-nav budget
link drop hop desync
PANEL 07 · SUBSURFACE — SONAR FINE, COMMS LOCKED OUT
Story

Here is the counterintuitive one. The boat's own sonar and torpedo ranging are fine — sound is so slow that a microsecond of clock error is a millimetre of range. But when it comes shallow to stream an antenna for its brief comms window, its nudged clock no longer matches the network's time-of-day schedule. It misses the window; it cannot enter the net; the tasking never arrives.

System

Underwater sound travels at ~1500 m/s, so timing maps to range at 1.5 mm per microsecond — acoustics are timing-robust. The fragile parts are all RF: comms-window alignment, the crypto/frequency-hop sequence keyed on time-of-day, and TDMA net entry — microsecond-to-millisecond fragile. Submerged, holdover drift (~µs/day) compounds it.

Algebra
acoustic: Δr = 1500 · Δt → 1µs = 1.5 mm
RF backhaul: 1µs = 300 m
net entry needs |Δt| < sync window

Same clock error: negligible to sonar, five orders of magnitude larger for anything RF-referenced.

1.5 mm/µs acoustic — robust
300 m/µs RF backhaul — fragile
~µs/day GPS-denied holdover
window missed net lockout
PANEL 08 · THE FRAGILITY LADDER
A timing error is only as bad as the tightest clock that consumes it.

Read left to right: an offset landing to the right of a system's mark exceeds that system's tolerance. Nanosecond-class consumers (emitter geolocation, coherent radar) break at any microsecond offset — bounded or not. Microsecond-class consumers (grid PMUs, tactical fine-sync, telecom) sit right at the boundary. Millisecond-and-coarser consumers (net slotting, track fusion, sonar) only feel a large offset. The green line is where the Schumann bound holds the clock; the red sweep is where an unchecked attacker walks it. Note where the bound falls: it is not left of everything — it still exceeds the PMU and geolocation budgets, which is why the next act's alarm matters as much as the bound.

This is not hypothetical

Real timing disruptions, real operational impact — the mechanism above has already played out in the open.

GPS SVN-23
A decommissioned satellite pushed a ~13 µs UTC error onto part of the constellation for 13 h 45 m. It knocked BBC digital radio off air and disrupted telecom networks — from thirteen microseconds.

IV

The Signal Beneath

There is a clock the adversary does not own — one that arrives by physics, not by fiber, and is the same everywhere on Earth.

PANEL 09 · THE EARTH IS RINGING
Story

Lightning strikes tens of times a second, everywhere on the planet, and the gap between the ground and the ionosphere rings like a bell. That ringing — the Schumann resonance — is present at every point on Earth at the same instant. Two stations thousands of kilometers apart hear the same note.

System

It's a globally shared, extremely-low-frequency magnetic signal in the 6–25 Hz band. No one transmits it, so no one can silently substitute it. Its cross-node coherence gives two separated receivers a common physical time reference that owes nothing to the packet network.

Algebra
B(t) shared, coherent across baseline L
phase(f) common ∀ nodes, 6–25 Hz

The fiducial is a physical field, not a packet. To move it, an adversary would have to move the field itself at both nodes — not merely hold a message on a wire.

PANEL 10 · THE FIDUCIAL RIDES IN THE CONTENT
Story

Here's the turn. The old reference lived in when a packet arrived — the one thing the delay box controls. The new reference lives in what the message carries: a stamp of the shared waveform. Hold the packet all you like; the note it captured already happened, identically, at both ends.

System

Moving the fiducial out of the arrival-time domain and into message content severs it from transport-layer manipulation. Delay is an attack on timing; here the timing information is data, and delaying data doesn't change what the data says.

Algebra
θ̂ S = f( phase B(t) ) — independent of arrival
∂θ̂ S / ∂δ = 0

The Schumann-derived offset has zero sensitivity to inserted packet delay. That immunity is what makes the next panel possible.

V

The Catch

Same adversary. Same δ. But now there are two clocks to compare — and the disagreement is exactly the attack.

PANEL 11 · TWO ESTIMATES DISAGREE
Story

The box does exactly what it did in Act II — and this time it leaves a fingerprint. The network clock says one thing; the Earth's clock says another. The distance between them isn't noise. It is the attack, made measurable for the first time.

System

Subtract the two offset estimates. The packet estimate carries the δ/2 pull; the Schumann estimate doesn't. The blind subspace from Panel 04 is no longer blind, because the second reference lies outside it — it supplies the missing equation.

Algebra
r = θ̂ pkt − θ̂ S = δ/2

The residual is the previously-aliased term, now standing on its own. One outside observable collapses an (n−1) blind space to something you can watch.

PANEL 12 · THE DETECTOR FIRES
Story

Small, steady disagreements don't panic the system — it expects a little jitter. But a persistent one-sided pull builds up, and once it clears the line, the operator gets what the whole network could never give in Act II: a clear, early "your time is being tampered with."

System

A CUSUM change-detector accumulates the residual and trips at a threshold set for a chosen false-alarm rate. And if the reference itself is jammed or lost, coherence simply drops — which registers as an integrity event, not a missed alarm. The failure mode is safe.

Algebra
S k = max(0, S k−1 + (r k − b))
alarm when S k ≥ h

Detection delay scales with log(mean-time-to-false-alarm)/bias. A sustained δ/2 is exactly the bias this test is built to catch.

PANEL 13 · THE DAMAGE IS BOUNDED
From unbounded and invisible → bounded and flagged.

The literature's own conclusion was that no bound was derivable from the node's observables. With the outside reference, anything below the detection floor is held to a finite envelope — tens of microseconds in these measurements — and anything above it raises an alarm. That trade, silence for a bound, is the entire contribution.

Where the threat relocates — read this before you believe it

The monitor is a genuine defense, not a force field — and honesty requires naming its limits:

  • The bound is not a universal “safe.” The held residual (~77 µs) still exceeds nanosecond-class tolerances (emitter geolocation, coherent radar) and even the PMU 1%-TVE budget (26.5 µs). For those consumers the protection is the integrity alarm, not the bound — and detection fires on a bias far finer than the microsecond-scale channel floor (the channel estimation bound στ and the detector’s pre-alarm bias θdet differ by ~3 orders of magnitude).
  • Sub-threshold delay. An adversary who stays under the detection floor is not caught — but is held to the finite bound instead of running the clock away freely. The floor is the price; the bound is the payoff.
  • Matched near-field injection. An attacker who reproduces the shared field at the node with the right phase can survive loop closure. This doesn't defeat the idea — it relocates the attack from a remote wire tap to physical near-field access at the site.
  • Denial, not deception. Jamming or removing the signal doesn't forge a false "now"; it removes coherence, which the monitor reports as a fault. Loss of the reference is a detected event, not a bypass.
You cannot eliminate
the delay attack.
You can bound it,
and see it.

The wire is blind by theorem. The signal beneath it is not owned by the adversary. Put the timing fiducial where delay can't reach — in the content, in the physics — and the invisible becomes a number you can watch cross a line.
